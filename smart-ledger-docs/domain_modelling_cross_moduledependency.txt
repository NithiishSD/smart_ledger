Step 4.9 — Define Cross-Module Dependencies

Now we identify how the different SmartSilk modules depend on one another.

This is important because SmartSilk is not a collection of independent CRUD modules. A single business action can affect multiple modules.

For example:

Receiving raw silk affects purchasing, inventory, supplier obligations, and dashboard visibility.

If we don't understand these dependencies now, the backend may become tightly coupled and difficult to maintain.

4.9.1 What is a Cross-Module Dependency?

A cross-module dependency exists when one module needs information or actions from another module.

Example:

Customer Order
      ↓
Needs raw silk availability
      ↓
Inventory Module

The order module depends on inventory information, but it should not directly manipulate inventory database tables.

Instead:

It should request inventory allocation through a defined service/interface.

Our current modules are:

1. Supplier & Purchase Management

We need to understand their dependency direction.

4.9.4 Supplier & Purchase → Inventory
Dependency

The Purchase module creates the expectation that material will enter the business.

The Inventory module records the material only when it is physically received.

Purchase Ordered
      ↓
Material Receipt
      ↓
Inventory Increased
Important distinction

Purchase should not directly update an arbitrary inventory balance.

Instead:

Purchase Module
      ↓
Material Received event/action
      ↓
Inventory Module records movement
Why?

Because:

Ordered material is not available stock
Partially received material must be supported
Returned material must be tracked
Inventory must reflect physical reality
4.9.5 Inventory → Customer Orders

Customer orders need to know whether sufficient material/product is available.

Example:

Customer orders 100 kg Silk Warp
             ↓
Order Module
             ↓
Checks finished inventory
             ↓
If unavailable:
Production or outsourcing required

The Order module may request:

Available finished stock
Allocatable stock
Material availability
Existing commitments

But it should not directly modify inventory.

Dependency type

Order depends on Inventory availability.

4.9.6 Customer Orders → Production

If an order cannot be fulfilled from finished inventory:

Customer Order
      ↓
Production Requirement
      ↓
Production Module

The production module needs:

Product type
Required weight
Warp specification, if applicable
Expected completion
Order reference
Important

An order should not own the entire production process.

Instead:

The order creates a requirement, while Production manages how the product is manufactured.

This allows production to also happen:

Ahead of demand
For general stock
For multiple orders
4.9.7 Customer Orders → Outsourcing

If internal production is unavailable:

Customer Order
      ↓
Outsourcing Requirement
      ↓
Outsourcing Module

But outsourcing may also happen without a specific customer order.

Example:

The owner sends material to a manufacturer ahead of peak demand.

Therefore:

Customer Order ──optional──→ Outsourcing Job

The relationship should not be mandatory.

4.9.8 Inventory → Production

Production depends heavily on inventory.

Before production starts, the system should validate:

Is enough raw silk available?
Is the material already allocated?
Is the material suitable for this product?
Is it already being used elsewhere?

Example:

Available raw silk = 70 kg
Production requires = 100 kg

The system should not allow the operation unless:

Additional material becomes available, or
Owner explicitly records an authorized exception.
Dependency
Production → Inventory allocation/consumption
4.9.9 Inventory → Outsourcing

When raw silk is sent to an external manufacturer:

Inventory
   ↓
Issue material
   ↓
External WIP

The Outsourcing module should request an inventory movement such as:

ISSUE_TO_OUTSOURCING

It should not simply subtract weight directly from a stock field.

When material returns:

External WIP
   ↓
Finished Warp Inventory

4.9.11 Customer Orders → Customer Payments

An order creates a customer receivable when payment is not fully received.

Customer Order
      ↓
Receivable
      ↓
Customer Payment
      ↓
Payment Allocation

The payment module needs:

Customer
Outstanding orders
Payment amount
Payment method
Allocation strategy

But the order module should not independently modify bank balances.

4.9.12 Customer Payments → Financial Ledger

This is a critical dependency.

Customer Payment
      ↓
Financial Transaction
      ↓
Cash / Bank Account

Example:

Customer pays ₹25,000 via UPI

Effects:

Customer outstanding decreases
Bank account increases
Financial transaction recorded
Payment method stored

This must be coordinated transactionally.

4.9.13 Supplier Purchase → Supplier Payables

A credit purchase creates a supplier obligation.

Purchase
      ↓
Supplier Payable
      ↓
Supplier Payment

If the purchase is cash-paid:

Purchase
      ↓
Cash/Bank decreases immediately

If credit-paid:

Purchase
      ↓
Payable created
      ↓
Later Supplier Payment

This means Purchase depends on financial classification, but should not directly implement the entire finance system.

4.9.14 Supplier Payments → Finance
Supplier Payment
      ↓
Financial Transaction
      ↓
Cash/Bank decreases

The supplier payment module determines:

Which supplier obligation is being settled
Which purchases are allocated
How much remains outstanding

The finance module records the actual money movement.

4.9.15 Expenses → Finance

Expense recording and money movement are related but distinct.

Example:

Expense Created
      ↓
Expense Classification
      ↓
Financial Transaction
      ↓
Cash/Bank Decrease

This allows future reporting to distinguish:

Operating expense
Outsourcing expense
Personal drawing
Loan interest
Other costs
4.9.16 Loans → Finance

Loan management owns the loan obligation.

Finance owns the actual money movement.

Loan Received
      ↓
Loan Principal Increases
      ↓
Bank/Cash Increases

For repayment:

Loan Repayment
      ↓
Principal / Interest Allocation
      ↓
Cash/Bank Decrease
Important separation
Loan Module:
"How much do we owe?"

Finance Module:
"Where did the money move?"
4.9.17 Chits → Finance

Chit management owns:

Chit identity
Contributions
Bidding
Payout
Remaining contributions

Finance records the physical cash movement.

Chit Contribution
      ↓
Chit Ledger
      ↓
Physical Cash Decrease
Chit Payout
      ↓
Chit Ledger
      ↓
Physical Cash Increase

Because you specified that chit transactions are cash-only, the Chit module must validate that rule.

4.9.18 Bills/Documents → Other Modules

Bills are supporting records attached to business transactions.

Possible dependencies:

Customer Order → Sales Bill
Purchase → Supplier Bill
Expense → Expense Document
Bank Transaction → Bank Statement
Refund → Adjustment Document

The Bills/Documents module should not become the source of financial truth.

For example:

A bill says ₹50,000, but that does not automatically mean ₹50,000 was paid.

Therefore:

Bill ≠ Payment
Bill ≠ Financial Transaction

The Dashboard module is a consumer of data, not an owner of core transactions.

4.9.21 Dependency Classification

Not all dependencies are equally strong.

Strong dependency

One module cannot perform its operation without another.

Example:

Production → Inventory

Production cannot consume material that inventory does not recognize.

Optional dependency

A module may operate with or without another module.

Example:

Customer Order → Outsourcing

An order may be fulfilled internally or from existing stock.

Reporting dependency

A module only reads data for analysis.

Example:

Dashboard → All Modules
Notification dependency

A module provides conditions to the notification engine.

Example:

Loan → Notifications
4.9.22 Recommended Dependency Direction

For a modular monolith, a clean direction could be:

                    Presentation/API
                           ↓
                    Application Services
                           ↓
       ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
       ↓                   ↓                   ↓
   Purchase            Sales/Orders        Finance
       ↓                   ↓                   ↓
   Inventory          Production          Loans/Chits
       └───────────────────┼───────────────────┘
                           ↓
                    Shared Domain Rules
                           ↓
                       PostgreSQL

However, avoid making every module directly call every other module.

For example, this is dangerous:

Purchase → Inventory → Finance → Orders → Production → Purchase

That creates circular dependencies.

Instead, use:

Application services
Clear interfaces
Domain events where appropriate
Shared transaction orchestration only at use-case boundaries

4.9.26 Key Architectural Conclusion

SmartSilk should be designed as a:

Modular monolith with clear domain boundaries and controlled cross-module interactions.

Not:

Every module directly edits every other module's tables

Instead:

Business Use Case
      ↓
Application Service
      ↓
Domain Validation
      ↓
Relevant Module Operations
      ↓
Financial/Inventory Consistency
      ↓
Database Transaction

For example:

RecordCustomerPayment() should orchestrate payment allocation and financial recording, rather than allowing the frontend to separately update payment, outstanding, and bank balance.

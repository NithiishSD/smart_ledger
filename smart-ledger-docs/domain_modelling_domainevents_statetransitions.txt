Step 4.8 — Define Domain Events & State Transitions

Now we define what business actions happen inside SmartSilk and what changes they cause.

This is different from lifecycles:

Lifecycle → current state of an entity
Domain event → something meaningful happened in the business
State transition → how the entity changes because of that event

Example:

Business Event:
Customer Payment Received

Effects:
- Payment recorded
- Bank/cash balance increases
- Payment allocated to outstanding order
- Customer outstanding decreases
- Order payment status may change
4.8.1 What is a Domain Event?

A domain event represents a meaningful business occurrence.

Examples:

PurchaseOrdered
MaterialReceived
MaterialReturned
CustomerOrderPlaced
ProductionStarted
ProductionCompleted
OutsourcingMaterialIssued
OutsourcingWarpReceived
CustomerPaymentReceived
SupplierPaymentMade
ExpenseRecorded
LoanReceived
LoanPrincipalRepaid
ChitContributionRecorded
RefundSettled

These events describe what happened, not merely what database operation occurred.

Bad event naming:

InsertPurchaseRow
UpdateInventoryColumn

Better:

MaterialReceived

Because the event expresses business meaning.

4.8.2 Purchase Events
Event: Purchase Ordered
PurchaseOrdered
Effects
Purchase record created
Supplier obligation may be created depending on payment terms
Expected material quantity recorded
Receiving status becomes ORDERED

Example:

Purchase:
180 kg
₹90,000
Credit purchase

At this point:

Expected material = 180 kg
Available inventory = unchanged
Supplier payable = ₹90,000

Important:

Ordering material does not mean it has physically entered inventory.

Event: Material Received
MaterialReceived
Effects
Material receipt created
Available inventory increases
Purchase received quantity increases
Purchase receiving status recalculated

Example:

Purchase ordered = 180 kg
Receipt received = 100 kg

Result:

Received = 100 kg
Remaining expected = 80 kg
Purchase status = PARTIALLY_RECEIVED

When another 80 kg arrives:

Purchase status = FULLY_RECEIVED
Event: Material Returned
MaterialReturned
Effects
Available inventory decreases
Receipt's returned quantity increases
Purchase/receipt return history is preserved
Supplier adjustment may be created

Example:

Received = 100 kg
Used = 1 kg
Returned = 99 kg

The system should not rewrite the original receipt as though 100 kg never arrived.

4.8.3 Customer Order Events
Event: CustomerOrderPlaced
CustomerOrderPlaced
Effects
Customer order created
Required product and weight recorded
Order becomes PLACED
Optional material requirement/commitment is generated
Event: CustomerOrderConfirmed
CustomerOrderConfirmed
Effects
Order becomes confirmed
Business commitment becomes active
Material availability may be checked
Production or allocation can be planned
Event: ProductionStartedForOrder
ProductionStartedForOrder
Effects
Order becomes IN_PROGRESS
Required material may be allocated
Order modification restrictions become stronger

This is important because your business rule says that once production starts, the customer should not freely change or cancel the order.

Event: ProductDelivered
ProductDelivered
Effects
Finished inventory decreases
Delivered quantity is recorded
Order becomes DELIVERED
Billing/payment eligibility may change

But remember:

Delivery does not necessarily mean payment has been received.

Event: OrderCompleted
OrderCompleted
Conditions

Usually:

Required product delivered
Any required final adjustments completed
Order is no longer operationally pending

The payment status should remain separately tracked.

4.8.4 Customer Payment Events
Event: CustomerPaymentReceived
CustomerPaymentReceived
Effects
Payment record created
Financial transaction created
Cash/bank balance increases
Payment allocated to outstanding orders
Customer outstanding recalculated
Relevant order payment status updated
Related overdue notification resolved if applicable

Example:

Customer owes:
Order A = ₹20,000
Order B = ₹30,000
Order C = ₹40,000

Payment received = ₹45,000

Oldest-first allocation:

Order A settled = ₹20,000
Order B partially settled = ₹25,000
Order B remaining = ₹5,000
4.8.5 Supplier Payment Events
Event: SupplierPaymentMade
SupplierPaymentMade
Effects
Payment recorded
Cash/bank decreases
Payment allocated to oldest supplier obligations
Supplier payable recalculated
Overdue reminder may be resolved

Example:

Purchase A payable = ₹40,000
Purchase B payable = ₹60,000
Payment made = ₹70,000

Allocation:

Purchase A = fully settled
Purchase B = ₹30,000 settled
Purchase B remaining = ₹30,000
4.8.6 Production Events
Event: ProductionStarted
ProductionStarted
Effects
Production batch becomes IN_PROGRESS
Raw silk is allocated or consumed depending on the chosen material model
WIP increases
Available raw silk decreases

Example:

Available raw silk = 200 kg
Production input = 80 kg

After event:

Available raw silk = 120 kg
Production WIP = 80 kg
Event: ProductionCompleted
ProductionCompleted
Effects
WIP decreases
Finished warp inventory increases
Finished weight recorded
Wastage recorded
Production batch becomes COMPLETED
Worker work records may be finalized

Example:

Input = 80 kg
Finished warp = 76 kg
Wastage = 4 kg

The system should preserve this reconciliation:

Input = Finished Output + Wastage + Difference

If there is unexplained difference, it should be visible rather than silently ignored.

4.8.7 Outsourcing Events
Event: OutsourcingJobCreated
OutsourcingJobCreated
Effects
Outsourcing job created
Manufacturer assigned
Expected raw material and product details recorded
Event: MaterialIssuedToManufacturer
MaterialIssuedToManufacturer
Effects
Available raw silk decreases
External WIP increases
Issued weight recorded
Job status becomes SENT_TO_MANUFACTURER or IN_PROGRESS

Example:

Available raw silk = 500 kg
Issued externally = 150 kg

Result:

Available raw silk = 350 kg
External WIP = 150 kg
Event: OutsourcedWarpReceived
OutsourcedWarpReceived
Effects
External WIP decreases
Finished warp inventory increases
Received weight recorded
Manufacturer payable calculated or updated
Job status becomes PARTIALLY_RECEIVED or FULLY_RECEIVED

Example:

Issued = 150 kg
Received = 143 kg
Difference = 7 kg

The difference should be visible for review.

4.8.8 Worker Events
Event: WorkRecorded
WorkRecorded
Effects
Worker work record created
Hours/kg and applicable historical rate stored
Earnings calculated
Worker payable increases

Example:

Rolling:
8 hours × ₹100 = ₹800
Event: WorkerPaid
WorkerPaid
Effects
Financial transaction created
Cash/bank decreases

Worker outstanding decreases

One payment may settle multiple work records.

Effects

Important:

An expense should not be treated as revenue reduction merely because money went out. It must be classified correctly.

4.8.15 Important Design Decision: Events vs Direct Updates

For the MVP, you do not necessarily need a full event-driven microservices architecture.

You can implement these as normal Spring Boot service operations inside database transactions.

For example:

recordCustomerPayment()

internally performs:

- Create payment
- Create financial transaction
- Allocate payment
- Recalculate outstanding
- Resolve notification

You can still think in terms of domain events without introducing Kafka or a complex event bus.

Recommended MVP approach
Spring Service Method
       ↓
Database Transaction
       ↓
Business State Changes
       ↓
Optional Internal Domain Event / Audit Record

This gives you clean domain thinking without unnecessary infrastructure.

              Validate Business Invariants
                           ↓

                           ↓

                Recalculate Derived Views

                Generate Notifications

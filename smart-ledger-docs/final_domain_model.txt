Step 4.11 — Finalize the Domain Model

This is the final stage of domain modeling.

Until now, we identified:

Domain concepts
Attributes

Lifecycles

Aggregate boundaries
Domain events
Cross-module dependencies

Now we freeze the approved domain model before moving to architecture and database design.

The important principle is:

After Step 4.11, we should not casually add entities during implementation. Any new concept must be treated as a deliberate requirement change.

4.11.1 Final Domain Model Structure

We will organize the model into logical domains rather than treating every entity as unrelated.

SmartSilk can be divided into these bounded modules:

1. Party Management
2. Procurement
3. Inventory and Material
4. Customer Orders and Sales
5. Production
6. Outsourcing
7. Workforce
8. Finance
9. Documents
10. Notifications
11. Reporting and Dashboard

This does not mean separate microservices.

It means logical boundaries inside one modular monolith.

4.11.3 Procurement Domain

This domain handles purchasing raw silk from suppliers.

Final entities
Purchase

Represents what the business ordered or bought from a supplier.

It answers:

What did we purchase, from whom, at what price, and under what terms?

It does not itself represent physically received inventory.

Material Receipt

Represents what physically arrived from a purchase.

It answers:

How much material actually arrived and when?

One purchase may have multiple receipts.

Purchase 1 ─── N Material Receipts
Purchase-related payment obligation

Supplier payable is not a separate manually maintained entity.

It is derived from:

Credit Purchases
− Supplier Payments
− Returns/Adjustments
Procurement flow
Supplier
   ↓
Purchase
   ↓
Material Receipt
   ↓
Material Movement
   ↓
Raw Silk Inventory
4.11.4 Inventory and Material Domain

This is one of the most important domains in SmartSilk.

The system should not treat inventory as merely a number stored in a table.

Inventory should represent the history of physical material movement.

Final core concept
Material Movement

Every change in material quantity must create a material movement.

Examples:

Material received
Material returned to supplier
Raw silk sold directly
Raw silk consumed in production
Raw silk sent to manufacturer
Finished warp received from manufacturer
Production output
Wastage
Customer return
Manual adjustment with reason
Material movement structure

Conceptually:

Material Movement
├── Movement ID
├── Date
├── Product/material type
├── Quantity
├── Movement type
├── Source reference
├── Related business operation
└── Notes/reason

Do not yet decide database columns. This is still the domain level.

Inventory states

Inventory is logically divided into locations/states:

AVAILABLE_RAW_SILK
INTERNAL_WIP
EXTERNAL_WIP
AVAILABLE_FINISHED_WARP
RETURNED_OR_REJECTED_MATERIAL
WASTAGE

These may be represented through movement types and location/state fields rather than separate entities.

Important decision: Raw Material Lot

A separate Raw Material Lot entity is not mandatory at this stage.

Why?

Because the current business primarily needs:

Total available raw silk
Material received
Material consumed
Material sent outside
Material returned
Wastage
Current external WIP

It does not necessarily require strict batch-level traceability for every individual silk bundle.

Therefore:

Do not force a lot-based model unless future traceability requirements justify it.

If later the business needs FIFO by purchase lot, supplier-quality tracking, or exact source tracing, Raw Material Lot can be introduced.

4.11.6 Payment Domain

Payment is a business settlement event.

It answers:

Who paid whom, how much, and for which obligation?

Final entities
Payment

Represents the actual settlement instruction or business payment.

Examples:

Customer payment
Supplier payment
Worker payment
Manufacturer payment
Refund settlement
Payment Allocation

Represents how a payment is applied to one or more obligations.

This is essential.

Payment 1 ─── N Payment Allocations

A payment allocation can reference:

Customer order
Supplier purchase
Work record
Outsourcing job
Other payable/receivable
Example
Payment P001 = ₹50,000

Allocations:
- Order O001 = ₹30,000
- Order O002 = ₹20,000

Without Payment Allocation, outstanding calculations become unreliable.

Advance payment

If a customer pays before an order is created or before the full amount is known, the payment should be marked as:

ADVANCE

It must not automatically be counted as completed sales revenue.

4.11.9 Workforce Domain
Final entities
Work Record

Represents work performed by a worker.

It answers:

What work did the worker perform, when, and how much was earned?

Worker Payment

This may be represented using the generic Payment entity with worker-related allocation.

For the MVP, a separate WorkerPayment entity is not strictly necessary if:

Worker payments are recorded through Payment
Work records have payment status
Payment allocations can link to work records
Important distinction
Work completed ≠ Money paid

Example:

Worker completed ₹8,000 worth of work
Only ₹5,000 paid
Remaining payable = ₹3,000

Historical rates must be preserved in the Work Record.

If the worker’s rate changes next month, old work records must not be recalculated.

4.11.14 Documents Domain
Final entity
Bill / Invoice

Represents a formal business document.

It may be associated with:

Purchase
Expense

GST transaction

A bill can exist independently of payment timing.

Document principle
Document ≠ Business Transaction ≠ Payment

A document supports evidence and compliance, but should not replace the underlying transaction.

4.11.17 Concepts Explicitly Not Made Independent Entities

To avoid unnecessary complexity, these should remain derived values or controlled concepts.

Do not create manually editable entities for:
Customer Outstanding
Supplier Outstanding
Cash Balance
Bank Balance
Available Inventory
Finished Stock Balance
External WIP Balance
Total Profit

These are calculated from source records.

Do not initially create separate entities for:
Raw Material Lot
Inventory Balance

Supplier Outstanding Record
Profit Record

They can be introduced later only if performance or traceability requires them.

4.11.19 Domain Model Freeze Rules

From this point onward:

Rule 1

Do not add an entity merely because it sounds technically useful.

Ask:

Does it represent an independent business concept, identity, lifecycle, or obligation?

Rule 2

Do not duplicate derived values.

For example, avoid independently storing:

customer.outstanding
supplier.outstanding
inventory.availableWeight

unless there is a carefully controlled projection/cache.

Rule 3

Do not mix business events and financial transactions.

Rule 4

Do not let one module directly manipulate every other module’s internal data.

Rule 5

Every inventory change must have a material movement.

Rule 6

Every actual money change must have a financial transaction.

Rule 7

Every settlement requiring allocation must preserve payment allocation history.

Rule 8

Historical rates, prices, and quantities must never be silently recalculated using current values.

# SmartSilk — Step 2: Business & Problem Analysis

**Document Status:** Business Discovery / Domain Understanding
**Purpose:** Establish a shared understanding of the existing silk business, its problems, processes, financial flows and desired business visibility before defining software requirements.

---

## 1. Business Overview

### 1.1 Business Domain

SmartSilk is intended for a **silk yarn trading and warp manufacturing business**.

The business has two major operational activities:

1. **Trading raw silk yarn**

   * Raw silk yarn is purchased from suppliers.
   * Some of the purchased yarn is sold directly to customers.

2. **Manufacturing warp from raw silk yarn**

   * Raw silk yarn is processed into warp.
   * Warp is primarily produced based on customer demand/orders.
   * Production may be performed internally or outsourced to other manufacturers when internal production capacity is occupied.

The business also involves substantial financial activity, including:

* Customer credit
* Supplier credit
* Customer payments
* Supplier payments
* Worker payments
* Business expenses
* Personal drawings
* Chit-fund investments
* Short-term loans
* Multiple bank accounts
* Cash transactions

Therefore, SmartSilk is **not simply an inventory or accounting application**.

Its primary purpose is to provide **end-to-end operational and financial visibility across the silk business**.

---

# 2. Business Objective

The current business relies heavily on manual ledger-based record keeping.

The primary objective of SmartSilk is to provide a system where the business can:

> **Track the movement of silk, production, sales, payments, expenses and financial commitments in one connected system and provide a clear view of the current position of the business.**

The system should help the business owner understand:

* What raw silk has been purchased
* How much raw silk is available
* How much is being processed
* How much warp has been produced
* How much warp/raw silk has been sold
* How much customers owe
* How much suppliers/workers are owed
* What payments are upcoming
* What expenses have occurred
* What loans are outstanding
* What chit-fund commitments exist
* Where material or financial losses may be occurring

---

# 4. Current Business Problems

## 4.1 Manual Ledger Dependency

A large number of transactions are recorded manually in physical ledger notebooks.

Information is distributed across different records, including:

* Customer ledgers
* Worker ledgers
* Job-worker ledgers
* Expense ledger
* Chit-fund records
* Other records

This makes it difficult to obtain an immediate consolidated view.

---

## 4.2 High Transaction Volume

The business has many transactions involving:

* Purchases
* Sales
* Partial payments
* Worker payments
* Expenses
* Loans
* Chit payments
* Investments

Manually maintaining these relationships becomes increasingly difficult as transactions accumulate.

---

## 4.3 Customer Credit Tracking

Customers may receive goods without paying the complete amount immediately.

There is:

* No fixed customer credit deadline
* Partial payments
* Full payments
* Payments made months later
* Payments that may relate to older orders rather than recent orders

Therefore, the business must continuously determine:

> How much has this customer paid and how much is still outstanding?

---

## 4.4 Payments Are Not Necessarily for the Latest Order

A customer may:

```text
Order 1
Order 2
Order 3
Order 4
```

and later make a payment that does not correspond to the latest order.

The current business practice is to settle outstanding orders in chronological order:

```text
Oldest unpaid order
        ↓
Next oldest unpaid order
        ↓
Next oldest unpaid order
```

This is an important business rule.

---

## 4.5 Delayed Final Bill Generation

A reference/dummy bill is maintained to understand:

* Order
* Weight
* Amount

However, the **final bill is generated only after the particular order has been fully paid**.

The bill may then be shared with the customer through WhatsApp when required.

Therefore:

> Delivery of goods and financial completion of an order are different events.

---

## 4.6 Supplier Payment Tracking

Supplier credit periods are short and vary between suppliers.

Some purchases are:

* Immediate cash purchases
* Credit purchases

Currently, the owner can often manage supplier payments from memory because:

* There are relatively few suppliers
* Credit periods are relatively short

However, this provides less systematic visibility than desired.

---

## 4.7 Production Visibility

The business currently records raw material and finished warp quantities, but does not fully reconcile:

```text
Raw silk input
       ↓
Processing
       ↓
Finished warp
       ↓
Wastage / variance
```

The owner wants SmartSilk to provide better visibility into material loss and production efficiency.

---

## 4.8 Wastage Visibility

The current process does not systematically determine whether:

```text
Raw material input
=
Finished warp
+
Wastage
```

The desired system should eventually allow material reconciliation and investigation of unexplained differences.

---

## 4.9 Distributed Financial Information

Financial information exists across different ledgers and records.

As a result, it is difficult to immediately determine the overall business position:

```text
Customer receivables
Supplier payables
Worker payables
Expenses
Loans
Chit commitments
Cash
Bank balances
```

---

## 4.10 Lack of Immediate Business Analysis

The owner currently focuses primarily on running the business rather than continuously analysing historical records.

The desired system should make it easier to answer:

* Where is money being spent?
* Where is material being lost?
* How much is currently receivable?
* How much is payable?
* How much money is committed?
* How much silk is available?
* How much has been converted into warp?
* How much has been sold?
* What is the current business position?

---

## 4.11 WhatsApp Bill/Document Handling

Bills and related documents are currently handled manually.

There is a requirement to make bill generation and WhatsApp sharing easier because customers may be located elsewhere.

Incorrect allocation could cause the wrong orders to appear unpaid or paid.

## Business/personal mixing

If personal drawings and business expenses are not distinguished, profitability analysis becomes unreliable.

## AI financial advice

Future AI features must not independently invent or modify financial facts.

## External integrations

WhatsApp and AI services can fail or become unavailable.

## Historical data migration

Existing notebook records may be incomplete or inconsistent when transferred into SmartSilk.

## User adoption

The system must be substantially easier than the existing notebook process, otherwise the owner may stop using it.

# 37. Core Business Goal

The most important goal can be summarized as:

> **Give the business owner complete, reliable and easy-to-understand visibility into the movement of raw silk, production, finished warp, sales, customer receivables, supplier/worker payables, expenses, cash, bank accounts, loans and investments so that he can make better operational and financial decisions.**

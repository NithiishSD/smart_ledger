4.5 Define Entity Lifecycles / States
4.5.1 First — an important distinction

There are two different things we need to model:

1. Entity state

What is the current business status?

Example:

Customer Order → IN_PROGRESS

2. Financial/material status

What has happened financially or physically?

Example:

Purchase → fully received
but supplier payment → still outstanding

These should not be unnecessarily combined.

For example:

Purchase
 ├── Receiving Status → FULLY_RECEIVED
 └── Payment Status   → OUTSTANDING

This is much better than having one giant state like:

PURCHASE_COMPLETED

because a purchase can be physically complete while financially unpaid.

4.5.2 Purchase Lifecycle

A purchase represents:

“We agreed to buy this material from a supplier.”

Lifecycle
CREATED / ORDERED
       ↓
PARTIALLY_RECEIVED
       ↓
FULLY_RECEIVED
       ↓
CLOSED

But returns can happen:

FULLY_RECEIVED
       ↓
PARTIALLY_RETURNED
       ↓
CLOSED

or:

FULLY_RECEIVED
       ↓
FULLY_RETURNED
Important

Payment is separate.

Example:

Purchase = ₹90,000
Received = 180 kg
Supplier payment = ₹0

The purchase is:

FULLY_RECEIVED

but financially:

OUTSTANDING

This is exactly why we shouldn't use a single lifecycle state for everything.

Proposed purchase-related statuses

Receiving status

ORDERED
PARTIALLY_RECEIVED
FULLY_RECEIVED
CANCELLED

Financial status

UNPAID
PARTIALLY_PAID
PAID
OVERDUE
4.5.3 Material Receipt Lifecycle

We separated Purchase and Material Receipt, so now the receipt itself needs a lifecycle.

Example:

Purchase P001
       │
       ├── Receipt R001 → 100 kg
       └── Receipt R002 → 80 kg
Receipt lifecycle
EXPECTED
   ↓
RECEIVED
   ↓
PARTIALLY_RETURNED
   ↓
CLOSED

Or:

RECEIVED
   ↓
FULLY_RETURNED
Why this matters

Suppose:

100 kg received
1 kg already used
99 kg returned due to quality issue

The system should be able to represent:

Received       = 100 kg
Consumed       = 1 kg
Returned       = 99 kg

rather than simply changing the original receipt to 0 kg.

That preserves traceability.

4.5.4 Customer Order Lifecycle

This is one of the most important lifecycles.

A customer order could go:

PLACED
   ↓
CONFIRMED
   ↓
IN_PROGRESS
   ↓
READY
   ↓
DELIVERED
   ↓
COMPLETED
Cancellation

An order can potentially be cancelled before completion:

PLACED ─────→ CANCELLED
CONFIRMED ──→ CANCELLED
IN_PROGRESS → CANCELLED

But cancellation rules need to depend on how much material/production has already been committed.

For example:

Customer orders 100 kg warp.

If nothing has been produced:

cancellation is easy.

If 80 kg has already been produced specifically for the order:

cancellation becomes a business/financial adjustment.

So later, 4.6 Business Invariants will define what is allowed.

4.5.5 Production Batch Lifecycle

For in-house production:

PLANNED
   ↓
IN_PROGRESS
   ↓
COMPLETED

Possible cancellation:

PLANNED → CANCELLED
IN_PROGRESS → CANCELLED

But completed production should generally not simply be deleted or changed.

Example:

Raw silk supplied = 100 kg
Finished warp = 96 kg
Wastage = 4 kg

Once completed, the system records the production result.

Important

COMPLETED does not mean:

“Everything was perfect.”

It means:

“This production activity has been completed and its material result has been recorded.”

4.5.6 Outsourcing Job Lifecycle

For outsourced warp:

CREATED
   ↓
SENT_TO_MANUFACTURER
   ↓
IN_PROGRESS
   ↓
PARTIALLY_RECEIVED
   ↓
FULLY_RECEIVED
   ↓
CLOSED

Possible return/adjustment can occur after receipt.

Example:

Raw silk sent = 200 kg

Received:
150 kg → first receipt
 45 kg → second receipt

Difference = 5 kg

The system should preserve those movements.

Financial status is separate
Job Status:
FULLY_RECEIVED

Payment Status:
UNPAID

Later:

Payment Status:
PAID
4.5.7 Payment Lifecycle

Payments are particularly important because of cheques and corrections.

Basic lifecycle:

RECORDED
    ↓
COMPLETED

For cheque payments:

RECORDED
    ↓
PENDING_CLEARANCE
    ↓
COMPLETED

If the cheque fails:

PENDING_CLEARANCE
    ↓
FAILED / BOUNCED

And if a completed transaction needs correction:

COMPLETED
    ↓
REVERSED
Important rule

A completed payment should not be silently edited or deleted.

Instead:

Original Payment
      ↓
Reversal / Correction
      ↓
Corrected Payment

This gives us auditability.

4.5.8 Financial Transaction Lifecycle

A financial transaction represents actual money movement.

For example:

Customer pays ₹50,000
        ↓
Financial Transaction
        ↓
Bank account +₹50,000

Basic:

PENDING
   ↓
CLEARED / COMPLETED

For bank/cheque transactions:

PENDING
   ↓
CLEARED

or:

PENDING
   ↓
FAILED

Correction:

COMPLETED
   ↓
REVERSED
Why this is critical

The dashboard should calculate available money from valid completed/cleared transactions.

A pending cheque shouldn't suddenly increase available bank balance.

4.5.9 Worker Work Record Lifecycle

A work record represents:

“This worker performed this work.”

Example:

Worker:
Ravi

Work:
Rolling

Hours:
8

Rate:
₹100/hour

Earnings:
₹800

Lifecycle:

RECORDED
   ↓
VERIFIED
   ↓
PAYABLE
   ↓
PAID

However, we don't necessarily need all four states in the MVP.

A simpler model can be:

RECORDED
   ↓
PAID

The important distinction is:

Work Record ≠ Worker Payment

A worker may have:

Week 1 work = ₹3,000
Week 2 work = ₹2,500
Week 3 work = ₹3,200

and one weekly payment can settle the relevant records.

4.5.10 Expense Lifecycle

Expenses are simpler:

RECORDED
   ↓
PAID

But if we support planned expenses later:

PLANNED
   ↓
RECORDED
   ↓
PAID

For MVP, we probably don't need PLANNED.

The important rule is:

An expense should only affect actual cash/bank balance when its corresponding financial transaction occurs.

4.5.11 Loan Lifecycle

Loan:

ACTIVE
   ↓
PARTIALLY_REPAID
   ↓
COMPLETED

Example:

Original Principal = ₹5,00,000

Current Principal:
₹5,00,000
      ↓
₹4,00,000
      ↓
₹2,00,000
      ↓
₹0

When principal becomes zero:

COMPLETED

But interest payments remain separate.

Example:

Loan
 │
 ├── Principal repayment
 ├── Interest payment
 ├── Principal repayment
 └── Interest payment

This will become important for financial reporting.

4.5.12 Chit Lifecycle

A chit can have:

ACTIVE
   ↓
PAYOUT_RECEIVED
   ↓
CONTRIBUTION_CONTINUING
   ↓
COMPLETED

But depending on the actual business process, we may simplify this.

Conceptually:

ACTIVE
   ↓
PAYOUT_RECEIVED
   ↓
COMPLETED

while individual monthly contributions are tracked separately.

The important thing is that:

Chit status should not replace the chit transaction history.

The history remains the source of truth.

4.5.13 Bill / Invoice Lifecycle

Bills require history preservation.

Recommended:

DRAFT
   ↓
ISSUED
   ↓
COMPLETED

If correction is required:

ISSUED
   ↓
CORRECTED

or:

ISSUED
   ↓
CANCELLED

The original document should remain available.

Example

Bill:

INV-102
₹85,000

Mistake found.

Don't:

INV-102 → edit silently → ₹80,000

Instead:

INV-102 → CORRECTED

New version:
INV-102-R1 → ₹80,000

The exact numbering/version strategy can be finalized later.

4.5.14 Refund Lifecycle

Refund:

REQUESTED
   ↓
APPROVED
   ↓
SETTLED

or:

REQUESTED
   ↓
REJECTED

But since this is an owner-controlled MVP, we can simplify to:

RECORDED
   ↓
SETTLED

The important part is that a refund should create the appropriate financial/material adjustment rather than simply modifying the original sale.

4.5.15 Notification Lifecycle

Notifications are slightly different.

Example:

Supplier payment due in 3 days.

Lifecycle:

PENDING
   ↓
SENT
   ↓
READ

But if the underlying issue is unresolved:

PENDING
   ↓
SENT
   ↓
UNRESOLVED
   ↓
REMINDER

Eventually:

UNRESOLVED
   ↓
RESOLVED

Example:

Supplier payment due
       ↓
Notification sent
       ↓
Supplier paid
       ↓
Notification resolved

This prevents unnecessary repeated alerts after the issue is already settled.

4.5.16 Customer / Supplier Lifecycle

These are relatively simple master entities.

Customer
ACTIVE
   ↓
INACTIVE

We should not delete customers if historical orders/payments exist.

Same for suppliers:

ACTIVE
   ↓
INACTIVE

An inactive supplier can still appear in historical purchases.

4.5.17 Financial Account Lifecycle

For cash/bank accounts:

ACTIVE
   ↓
CLOSED

Example:

Bank Account A
    ↓
CLOSED

Historical transactions remain.

Again:

Closed does not mean deleted.

4.5.18 Summary of Important Lifecycles
Entity	Main lifecycle
Purchase	Ordered → Partially Received → Fully Received → Closed
Material Receipt	Expected → Received → Returned/Closed
Customer Order	Placed → Confirmed → In Progress → Ready → Delivered → Completed
Production Batch	Planned → In Progress → Completed
Outsourcing Job	Created → Sent → In Progress → Received → Closed
Payment	Recorded → Pending/Cleared → Completed → Reversed
Financial Transaction	Pending → Cleared/Completed → Reversed
Work Record	Recorded → Paid
Expense	Recorded → Paid
Loan	Active → Partially Repaid → Completed
Chit	Active → Payout → Completed
Bill	Draft → Issued → Corrected/Cancelled
Refund	Recorded → Settled
Notification	Pending → Sent → Resolved
Customer	Active → Inactive
Supplier	Active → Inactive
Financial Account	Active → Closed
